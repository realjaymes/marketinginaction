1Technical foundations

Can search engines and AI crawlers reach, read and index the site?

11 steps

Re-run quarterly, and straight after any site change, redesign, or move to a new domain.

1

Crawl blockers high impact

Is anything on your side stopping search engines reaching your pages?

Why it matters. A page that cannot be crawled cannot rank, and no amount of content or link work changes that.

Where to find it
Google Search ConsolefreeScreaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

Pages marked non-indexable, plus any noindex tag or robots.txt rule blocking a page you want ranked

Good looks like

Zero pages blocked that you intend to rank.

If it fails

Remove the blocking directive, then request indexing in Search Console.

2

Index coverage high impact

How many of your pages has Google actually indexed?

Why it matters. Only Search Console can tell you what Google holds. Bing Webmaster Tools does the same for Bing, which is worth checking separately because Bing's index is one of the things ChatGPT search reads from.

Where to find it
Google Search ConsolefreeBing Webmaster Toolsfree
What to look at

Indexed pages against sitemap pages, and sitemap processing status

Good looks like

90% or more of submitted pages indexed, sitemap processed within the last 30 days.

If it fails

Resubmit the sitemap, fix blockers found in the previous step, and improve internal links to unindexed pages.

3

Broken pages and links

Are you sending crawlers and people to pages that error?

Why it matters. Google only crawls so many of your pages in a given period, and broken pages use up that allowance for nothing. It is worse when another site links to a page of yours that now errors, because the value of that link goes nowhere.

Where to find it
Screaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

Pages returning a server error or a not-found, and the internal links and images still pointing at them

Good looks like

No server errors at all. Under 1% of your internal links pointing at pages that no longer exist.

If it fails

Repoint or remove the broken links. Redirect any missing page that still has other sites linking to it.

4

Redirect chains

Do your redirects hop more than once before landing?

Why it matters. Each hop loses a little and slows the page. Chains usually build up quietly after a site move or redesign.

Where to find it
Screaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

Redirects that pass through an intermediate URL before landing, and internal links pointing at a redirect rather than the final page

Good looks like

No chain longer than one hop.

If it fails

Point every redirect straight at its final destination.

5

Canonical tags

Do your canonical tags point somewhere valid?

Why it matters. A canonical aimed at a redirect, a broken page or itself in a loop tells Google to index the wrong thing or nothing.

Where to find it
Screaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

Canonical tags pointing at a redirect, a broken page, another canonical, or looping back on themselves

Good looks like

Every canonical tag points, in one step, at a page that loads normally.

If it fails

Point each canonical at the live final URL.

6

Core Web Vitals, field

What did real visitors actually experience?

Why it matters. This is the data Google actually scores you on, because it comes from real visits to your site. The lab test in the next step is for working out what to fix. This one decides whether you passed.

Where to find it
Google Search ConsolefreePageSpeed Insightsfree
What to look at

Core Web Vitals report, mobile and desktop

Good looks like

75% or more of visits pass on every metric.

If it fails

Use the lab audit in the next step to find causes, fix them, then confirm here. Field data lags about 28 days.

7

Core Web Vitals, lab

What is making the page slow, specifically?

Why it matters. Field data tells you that you have a problem. Lab data tells you which resource caused it.

Where to find it
PageSpeed InsightsfreeChrome DevToolsfreeDataForSEO Lighthouse$0.005 per pageDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

The Lighthouse performance score, how long the main content takes to appear, and which scripts or stylesheets are holding up the render

Good looks like

Performance 90 or above on the page types that carry the most traffic.

If it fails

Work the Lighthouse opportunities in order of estimated saving, largest first.

8

Internal links and orphan pages

Can a crawler reach every page by following links from the homepage?

Why it matters. An orphan page is invisible regardless of quality, and internal links are the cheapest ranking lever you own outright.

Where to find it
Screaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

Pages with no internal links pointing at them, and how many clicks each important page sits from the homepage

Good looks like

No page left without internal links pointing at it. Every important page within three clicks of the homepage.

If it fails

Add a link to each stranded page from a relevant parent or hub page.

9

Structured data

Do your pages carry valid structured data, of the right type?

Why it matters. Structured data drives rich results and gives AI systems an explicit description rather than a guess.

Where to find it
Google Rich Results TestfreeSchema Markup ValidatorfreeGoogle Search ConsolefreeScreaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

Whether each template carries structured data, whether it validates, and whether the type matches what the page actually is

Good looks like

Every page type carries markup, no validation errors, and the type matches what the page actually is.

If it fails

Fix errors first, then check the type is right. The crawl proves the markup exists. The Schema Markup Validator proves it is valid. Only the Rich Results Test proves Google will actually use it.

10

HTTPS and mobile

Is every page secure and usable on a phone?

Why it matters. Indexing is mobile-first, so the phone version is the version that counts.

Where to find it
Screaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

Pages not on HTTPS, secure pages linking out to insecure ones, missing mobile viewport settings, and unusually heavy pages

Good looks like

Everything on HTTPS, no secure page linking to an insecure one, and the mobile viewport tag set on every page type.

If it fails

Force HTTPS and repoint internal links at the secure URL.

11

Hreflang

If you serve more than one country or language, are the tags correct and reciprocal?

Why it matters. Broken hreflang sends the wrong language version to the wrong market, or collapses them into one.

Where to find it
Hreflang Tags Testing ToolfreeScreaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesManual checkfree
What to look at

Reciprocal tags, valid language and region codes, a self-referencing tag on each page

Good looks like

Each language version lists every other one, including itself, and a default version is set for everyone else.

If it fails

Rebuild the set so each page lists every alternative version including its own.

2On-page and content

Do the pages answer what buyers actually ask, in the format the results reward?

15 steps

Revisit when you publish new pages or rewrite old ones, and review the whole section quarterly.

1

Buyer question bank high impactflags missing sources

What do {audience} actually ask about {use_case}, in their own words?

Why it matters. Every step after this one uses the questions you collect here. If they all came from one place, you inherit that one source's blind spots, and every later answer looks confident while being wrong.

AlsoAsked and AnswerSocrates are the free route to the questions people actually type. A single search-results lookup per seed phrase returns people-also-ask questions, related searches, forum discussions and the AI overview together, for about $0.002.

At least two different sources, one of them your own evidence. Every question carries where it came from.

2

Keyword set with volume and difficulty

Why it matters. Without volume and difficulty next to each term, every content decision is a guess. Most sites lose a year competing for terms their domain cannot win, when a lower-difficulty tier with real demand sits untouched next to it.

Where to find it

Good looks like

Every target term carries a volume and a difficulty, and the list is filtered to difficulty your current authority can win.

If it fails

Start from your seed terms, expand them with a keyword tool, and pull volume and difficulty for each. Then cut everything above the difficulty your domain rank supports and work up from the bottom.

3

Title tags

Does every page have a unique, well-formed title?

Why it matters. The title is still the single strongest on-page signal and the line people decide on in the results.

Where to find it
Screaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

Pages with no title, titles too short or too long, and the same title used on more than one page

Good looks like

Every page unique, under 60 characters, primary term near the front.

If it fails

Rewrite duplicates and overlong titles, starting with the pages that already get impressions.

4

Meta descriptions

Does every page have a unique description that earns the click?

Why it matters. It does not rank the page, but it moves click-through rate, which is free traffic at a position you already hold.

Where to find it
Screaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

Pages with no description, and the same description used on more than one page

Good looks like

Every page unique, between 120 and 160 characters.

If it fails

Write descriptions for the pages with high impressions and low click-through rate first.

5

Headings

Does every page have one clear H1 and a sensible heading order?

Why it matters. Headings are how both crawlers and AI extractors work out what a page is about and which parts answer what.

Where to find it
Screaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

Pages with no H1, with more than one, or with headings that skip levels

Good looks like

Exactly one H1 per page, describing the page rather than the brand.

If it fails

Add the missing H1 and demote any duplicates.

6

Thin pages

Which pages have too little on them to rank for anything?

Why it matters. Thin pages dilute the site and rarely earn their crawl. They are usually cheaper to merge or remove than to improve.

Where to find it
Screaming Frog SEO Spiderfree up to 500 pagesDataForSEO On-Page$0.00015 per page crawled
What to look at

Pages carrying very little text for their size, low word counts, and poor readability scores

Good looks like

No page you want indexed sitting below the word count that page type needs.

If it fails

Merge, expand or remove. A page worth keeping is worth finishing.

7

Experience, expertise and trust signals high impact

Can a reader, a search engine or an AI assistant tell who wrote this and why they should be believed?

Why it matters. For anything touching health, money, safety or law, this is the factor that decides whether a page ranks at all, and it is the same factor that decides whether an AI assistant will quote it. Anonymous content with no sources and no named author loses to a worse page with a named expert behind it.

Where to find it
Manual checkfreeGoogle, in an incognito windowfreeThe brand's own recordsfree
What to look at

Named authors with real credentials and a biography page, a named reviewer on sensitive pages, publication and updated dates, outbound citations to authoritative sources, an editorial policy, and first-hand experience visible in the writing

Good looks like

Every page that matters names its author, shows a published and updated date, cites its sources, and on sensitive subjects names a qualified reviewer.

If it fails

Name the author and give them a real biography page. On anything about health, money, safety or law, name a qualified reviewer and publish an editorial policy. Cite an authoritative source for every claim, and let first-hand experience show rather than claiming expertise.

8

Depth against what already ranks

Do your pages cover what the pages already ranking cover?

Why it matters. A page can be long and still miss half of what the question needs. Search engines and AI assistants both favour the page that answers the whole question, not the longest one.

Where to find it

Good looks like

If it fails

Add the missing subjects to the page you already have rather than starting a new one, and keep your own numbers and examples in rather than rewriting to match everyone else.

9

Content angle against what already ranks

Does your page say something the ranking pages do not?

Why it matters. Matching what already ranks earns you the right to compete, not to win. When every page on the first results page makes the same argument in the same format, the one that takes the position is the one with a better angle or better execution.

Where to find it
Manual checkfreeGoogle, in an incognito windowfreeDataForSEO SERP$0.002 per keyword
What to look at

Good looks like

Your page takes a defensible angle the ranking pages do not, in the format the results reward, with your own data, examples or experience behind it.

If it fails

Read the first ten results and write down the angle each one takes. Pick the angle nobody has covered, or pick the most common one and out-execute it with first-hand detail they cannot copy.

10

Striking distance keywords high impact

11

12

Topical authority and cluster coverage

Do your pages cover the whole of {category}, or scattered pieces of several topics?

Why it matters. Search engines and AI assistants both reward the site that owns a subject rather than the one with a page about it. A pillar page with supporting pages around it, all linked together, beats the same word count spread across unrelated topics.

Where to find it

If it fails

Pick the topic you most want to own, build the pillar page, then fill the cluster around it before starting a second topic. Half-built clusters win nothing.

13

14

Re-run quarterly. Other sites link to you slowly, so checking monthly tells you nothing new.

If something moved, do we know why?

Only when something drops. Skip it entirely the rest of the time.

1
