Sound familiar

You keep applying. Nobody keeps replying.

You can do the work. You have done it for clients in Lagos, for your employer, for friends who paid you small money because they trusted you. Your portfolio is not bad. Your CV has been rewritten so many times you know it by heart.

And still, nothing.

You apply on LinkedIn on Monday. You apply on Indeed on Tuesday. Your inbox fills with "We have received your application" and then goes quiet. Three weeks later a rejection comes from a no-reply address, or nothing comes at all.

The 25th is coming. Your last application still says "received."

If you freelance, it looks different but feels the same. Your local clients pay in naira, and the naira keeps losing value while your rate stays the same. On Upwork you send a proposal into a job post that already shows 50 or more proposals, and the buyer who replies wants the work for less than your rate.

Meanwhile your mates keep announcing foreign roles and dollar clients on X, and you start to wonder if they know something you don't.

They usually do. They know where to look.

The real reason

You are fishing in the same pond as everyone else.

When a remote job goes up on the big boards, a crowd sees it at the same time. Most of them apply through the same portal with the same kind of CV. Software often screens the pile before a person reads it, so the person who decides may never see your work.

Some of those companies also cannot hire in Nigeria at all. Payroll, tax and employment law can stop them, even when the post says "remote." You can send them the best application of your life and the answer will still be no, because the decision was made before you applied.

So more applications will not fix it. A better CV will not fix it either, when the right person never sees it.

If you can already do the work, the fix sits in where you look, who you reach and what you send. All three can be learned.
